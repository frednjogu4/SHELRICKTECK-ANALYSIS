Instrument info

    Market state: what the chart looks like on four timeframes
    Trend, structure and volatility on the 4-hour, 1-hour, 15-minute and 5-minute chart, each read from finished candles and shown with the figures it was read from. It is a description, not a signal: the Buy, Sell and Wait of the signal pages are computed without it.
    Not read yet
    How each line is read
    • Trend. Uptrend: EMA 20 above EMA 50 above EMA 200, the close above EMA 50, and ADX 14 at 25 or more. Downtrend: the reverse. Range: ADX below 20, whatever order the averages are in. Unclear: everything else.
    • Structure. The last two swing highs and the last two swing lows. A swing high is a candle whose high stands above the five candles before it and is not passed by the five after it, so the newest swing is at least five candles old and never moves once it is drawn.
    • Volatility. ATR 14 of the last finished candle against the 200 candles before it: Quiet when fewer than 20 in 100 of them were smaller, High from 80, Extreme from 95.
    • Finished candles only. The candle still forming is never used, so a state changes at most once per candle of its timeframe. Times are Nairobi time (EAT). Index markets are not offered here: this app has no intraday candles for them.

    Signal Analyzer

    Multi-timeframe confluence (1m / 5m / 1h) into one validated decision with risk levels.

    How the confidence score is computed (quality score — NOT a probability of profit)
    Technical confluence score, 0–100: 15 base + 7 per confirming timeframe (up to 5) + up to 25 for bias strength (5×|avg bias|, capped) + 20 when the multi-timeframe signal is confirmed; overall cap 93 (WAIT signals capped at 45). Index CFD reads use 50 + 8×|signal score|, capped 90. Formula is fixed and shown here so the number is explainable and stable — it is a setup-quality/confluence score, not a calibrated chance of profit.
    It then moves with the market. A BUY or SELL is followed from the price it was first read at: up to 30 points come off as the live price moves from that entry toward the stop, and up to 15 are added as it moves toward target 1 (a move in the signal's direction uses up part of the target distance, so the score rises half as fast as it falls). The sentence under the score gives the distance in pips (dollars for gold) and when the signal was first read. At the stop or at target 1 that reading is over and the next analysis starts a new one. Daily index readings have no entry, stop or target, so their score moves only when a daily vote changes. Alerts and Sniper filters use the score as read. In the PC app with Exness connected, a reading made from Exness candles is measured at the Exness price of its latest analysis, never at a Deriv price, so it moves at each analysis instead of at each tick.
    One reading per finished 5-minute candle. A reading is made from finished candles only, as they stood at the last 5-minute close. A market can therefore change its reading at most once per 5-minute candle, and looking again in between changes nothing. Each card says which close it was read at.
    What the score has been worth, measured. In replays of this rule on historical 1-minute prices, a new BUY or SELL reached target 1 before its stop 37–41% of the time, depending on the market (gold, January 2020 to November 2026: 39.6% of 76,694). 40% is needed to break even before the spread, and signals with a higher score did no better. The line under each score gives the figure for that market. It is a measurement of the past, not a forecast.

        Calculator Lab — risk education
        Every tool here is educational and hypothetical: you supply the assumptions, the math is shown, nothing is sent to any broker, and nothing here predicts or guarantees anything. Market history comes from the live Deriv public feed and is labelled per panel. Past behaviour of a market never guarantees its future.
        1 · Percentage change
        How much did a value move, and what does it take to move back? Notice the asymmetry: a −50% move needs +100% to recover.
        Method: (to − from) ÷ |from| × 100. Recovery % = what the “to” value needs to return to “from”.
        2 · Hypothetical profit & loss
        Your assumptions only — enter an imagined entry/exit and costs. This is arithmetic on your numbers, not a forecast, and it never places an order.
        Method: gross = (exit − entry) × units × direction; costs = fees on both sides' notionals + slippage × 2 sides; margin = notional ÷ leverage. Leverage multiplies gains and losses alike.
        3 · Historical drawdown (real market history)
        Buy-and-hold drawdown from actual candles: how far the price fell below its running peak, and for how long. Limitations: one instrument, one period, one granularity — past drawdown does not bound future drawdown.
        No history loaded yet.
        Source: —
        4 · Volatility comparison (real market history)
        Realized (statistical) volatility from actual candle returns, annualized so different instruments are comparable. Limitations: a statistical description of the past — not a prediction, and it changes with every new candle.
        Method: stdev of log returns × √(periods/year). Data: Deriv public feed, last 400 candles per instrument.
        What leverage, fees and slippage really do
        • Leverage borrows buying power: 20× turns a 1% market move into a 20% move on your margin — in either direction. Losses can exceed the margin you posted.
        • Fees are charged on every side of every trade whether the idea worked or not; high-frequency plans must first overcome their own costs.
        • Slippage is the gap between the price you wanted and the price you got — it widens in fast markets and around news.
        • Losses are part of trading: no calculator, signal, or system eliminates them. Anyone promising otherwise is not being honest with you.
